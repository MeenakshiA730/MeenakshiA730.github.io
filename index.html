Consumer perception of eco-friendly and sustainable products

Final-year capstone, team of four | Supervisor: Dr. Sridhar D

My role: I built the main CatBoost classifier and the hybrid SBERT + CatBoost code, and created the Power BI dashboard and data visualizations.

Try the live demo on Hugging Face

Read the code on GitHub

  • Set out to close the gap between environmental concern and what people actually buy: how aware consumers are, what they will pay for sustainable options, and how demographics shape their choices.
  • Surveyed 1,008 consumers. Turned their open-ended answers into 384-dimensional Sentence-BERT embeddings (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) and merged them with encoded survey answers, demographics, text length and a sentiment score.
  • Trained a CatBoost classifier on that combined feature set to predict eco-friendly purchase intent, using a stratified 80:20 split and 5-fold cross-validation.
  • Built a Power BI dashboard for awareness, price sensitivity, demographics and consumer segments, and deployed the model as an interactive web service.

What the data showed: 54% of respondents had already bought an eco-friendly product, but only 22% would pay more for one. Younger (18 to 34) and higher-income respondents were the most receptive, and environmental concern and personal health were the top motivations.
